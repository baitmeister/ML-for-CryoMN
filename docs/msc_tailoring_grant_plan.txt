AI-guided tailoring of serum-free CryoMN formulations for MSC delivery

This project will develop a data-efficient approach to tailor cryomicroneedle (CryoMN) formulations for bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (BM-MSCs) and adipose-derived MSCs (AD-MSCs). MSCs offer therapeutic potential for tissue repair and immune modulation. Their clinical relevance is reinforced by the FDA approval of Ryoncil, a bone marrow-derived MSC therapy for pediatric steroid-refractory acute graft-versus-host disease. The study will investigate whether existing knowledge can guide formulation tailoring with limited additional experiments. Reference: https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-mesenchymal-stromal-cell-therapy-treat-steroid-refractory-acute-graft-versus-host

The methodology will combine transfer learning with Bayesian optimization. Transfer learning will carry information from the existing model, developed from 198 literature formulations and 106 wet-lab observations using human umbilical cord-derived MSCs, into the new BM-MSC and AD-MSC tasks. Specifically, we will use Gaussian process residual adaptation: the existing statistical model will provide a starting prediction of cell survival for each recipe, and small correction models will adjust these predictions using new measurements. This extends the baseline-plus-correction approach already used in the repository. The information transferred is the learned relationship between ingredient concentrations and survival; its relevance to each new preparation will be tested. Reference for the original model and validation study: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19677v1

Approximately six formulations will first be tested on both MSC preparations. An existing serum-free ectoin, ethylene glycol, and human serum albumin formulation will serve as the shared reference. The panel will include reference controls and modest changes in ethylene glycol, trehalose, and albumin, balancing promising predictions with informative variations. Cell loading, needle geometry, fabrication, freezing, and thawing conditions will remain consistent. Measurements will include post-thaw viability, subsequent cell recovery, and intact patch formation. Independent preparation repeats and matched unfrozen-cell controls will help distinguish formulation effects from handling variation.

For each first-round recipe, we will calculate the difference between measured survival and the existing model's prediction. A separate small Gaussian process model will be fitted to these differences for each MSC preparation, focusing on the three ingredients being varied. The resulting prediction will be the original prediction plus the preparation-specific correction. For example, if the original model predicts 70% survival and a BM-MSC experiment measures 55%, the observed correction is minus 15 percentage points. This is an illustration, not an experimental result. Across recipes, the correction models will learn how such differences depend on composition. Fine-tuning here means fitting these correction models while retaining the established source model. This approach belongs to Gaussian process transfer learning. Methodological reference: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/tighineanu22a.html

Bayesian optimization will then nominate approximately three additional formulations per cell type, alongside repeat reference measurements. It will rank recipes by expected improvement, a measure combining the opportunity to improve survival with uncertainty about the outcome. The selection will therefore test promising recipes while investigating alternatives that could outperform them. Adaptation will focus on survival, the endpoint supported by the original model; recovery and patch integrity will remain experimental selection criteria. Predictions will be recorded before second-round testing. Methodological reference: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02811

The expected cost benefit comes from avoiding a broad repeat screen of ingredient combinations already investigated. Existing relationships provide an informed starting point, while new experiments estimate how the tested preparations differ. Restricting the search to an established formulation platform and selecting informative recipes should reduce cell consumption, reagent use, and laboratory time. We will compare the adapted model with the unchanged model and a model trained only on the new measurements, using the same first-round data and independently measured second-round outcomes. Prediction accuracy and identification of successful formulations at the same experimental budget will assess the value of transfer. Actual preparation counts, cell consumption, and costs will be recorded; the pilot will establish whether transfer supports efficient tailoring, while a quantified reduction in experiments would require a prospective comparison with screening without transfer.

The leading formulations will then be independently prepared and tested on both cell preparations against the shared reference. This comparison will establish whether separate recipes offer a reproducible advantage or whether one formulation serves both preparations. Finalists will undergo delivery testing in ex vivo porcine skin, with confirmation of viable cells below the skin surface and comparison of delivered-cell recovery. Standardized photographs and matched controls will support screening for visible tissue injury. Gross appearance will serve as an initial tolerability screen; it cannot establish absence of microscopic necrosis. Published CryoMN cell-delivery methods provide a relevant foundation: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41427-024-00531-1

The expected outcome is a reusable workflow for developing serum-free candidate formulations through a small, model-guided experimental programme. Success will combine improved recovery relative to the shared reference, usable patches, and demonstrated viable-cell delivery. The pilot will use one donor per MSC source, so conclusions will apply to the tested preparations. Multi-donor validation, immune-function testing, and broader tissue-safety assessment will follow in subsequent studies.
