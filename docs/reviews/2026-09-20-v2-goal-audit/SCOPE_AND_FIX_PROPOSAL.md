**Scope correction and prioritized fix proposal — 20 September 2026**

The repository's task is to find useful trade-offs between post-thaw viability and nominal terminal axial compression force at +0.8 mm after the registered sustained 1 N trigger. Ex-vivo penetration validation of a later top-X shortlist is outside this computational project's scope. Approximately ten campaign rounds remain. This clarification supersedes the original review's treatment of downstream penetration/function testing as missing repository functionality and its overall 40% confidence assessment.

For this narrower computational task, my revised confidence is **75%**, a subjective assessment of implementation suitability, not a success probability or calibrated confidence in individual predictions. The change reflects the corrected scope and verification of the live acquisition path; no production defects have been fixed by this review.

The terminal-force endpoint remains the declared optimization target. I am not recommending another objective, additional GP input variables, a change in control-informed GP strategy, an early full-mechanics switch, or elimination of code whose compatibility role is uncertain. Greater mechanical resistance is a reasonable motivation for later penetration testing under fixed geometry/application conditions; proving that relationship is not a requirement for completing this repository's present task. Terminal compression force and stiffness remain physically distinct quantities, but that distinction does not require changing the agreed two-objective project.

**Responses to the seven annotations**

1. **Scope:** accepted. The original scientific-gap discussion extended beyond the computation-only remit. Ex-vivo penetration testing and delivered-cell validation belong to the later experimental stage. The code should continue to optimize the two declared endpoints.

2. **Remaining rounds:** the limited mechanical/control sample is an expected campaign stage, not itself a software defect or a reason to change models now. Continue collecting evidence. The verified data-handling defects matter because they could compromise those remaining rounds.

3. **Future-batch coverage:** confirmed as evaluation-only. Production uses `BatchGP.predict()` → `posterior(x)` without a future batch or fresh measurement noise, and the qLogNEHVI bundle exposes the same latent formulation posterior. Future-batch/measurement forecasts are generated by the comparison/evaluation workflow. Coverage results are not acquisition inputs or automatic model-promotion criteria. Historical batch effects and residual noise still influence the fitted latent posterior; excluding fresh-batch noise from acquisition does not mean ignoring batch effects during training.

4. **Operational GP:** confirmed. The recorded decision uses the control-informed, campaign-only viability GP with additive batch effects, coupled to the existing comparable-terminal-force mechanical GP. This is live in Group 11. The inability to identify every physical source of variation is not a recommendation to add those variables or change this model.

5. **Mechanical proxy:** the original universal-penetration-threshold caveat is not a missing computation requirement under the clarified scope. Preserve the terminal endpoint and defer application validation to the planned ex-vivo work.

6. **Group 11 endpoint activity:** both objectives are already active in hybrid candidate scoring and mechanical acquisition. The exclusion is a separate evaluation-policy gate: `formal_phase: mechanics_enabled` in the evaluation YAML. The evaluator applies it to overall `evaluation_eligible`, so it suppresses mechanical errors from both formal and ordinary supplementary cohort summaries during bootstrap/hybrid. The message `endpoint_not_active` is misleading: it means outside that evaluation phase, not unused by the optimizer. Full mechanics need not be active for mechanical BO scoring to operate in hybrid.

7. **What the interval observation meant:** 7.386 points is uncertainty about the follow-up recipe's expected formulation response; 14.635 points includes variability of a future batch/measurement. Both can be correct for different purposes. My concern is reporting interpretation, not that the acquisition should use 14.635. Keep latent uncertainty for selection. If measuring future-outcome interval calibration, use the already frozen future forecasts and label the interval kind explicitly. Separately, a boundary-capped selection SD should not be described as an untouched raw GP SD. This is a reporting/provenance improvement, not a reason to change selected candidates or add noise to acquisition.

**Thresholds are not required**

A multi-objective optimizer can seek a Pareto frontier and use hypervolume improvement without minimum acceptable viability or force. The existing `(0%, 0 N/needle)` hypervolume reference is a comparison baseline and influences which frontier improvements are valuable; it is not an acceptance requirement. There is no requirement to supply new acceptance thresholds before full mechanics solely to make the optimizer function.

A unique “best balance” eventually needs a shortlist preference, because several non-dominated formulations may make different trade-offs. That preference can be chosen when selecting top X for ex-vivo work. Hard thresholds would change the problem into one with explicit performance constraints, which is optional. Leave the current unset thresholds unchanged.
